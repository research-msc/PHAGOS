Abstract

Carbapenem-resistant Acinetobacter baumannii is a critical antimicrobial-resistance threat, yet identifying bacteriophages with suitable host specificity remains labor-intensive. Receptor-binding proteins (RBPs), which mediate initial phage recognition of bacterial surfaces, may contain sequence signals useful for computational host-association screening.

This study developed PHAGOS, a computer-based framework for prioritizing host-unassigned bacteriophage RBPs for possible A. baumannii association. PHAGOS used 886 unique known-host RBPs, including 64 A. baumannii-associated proteins, represented by nucleotide/codon, amino-acid, and physicochemical features under similarity-aware machine-learning evaluation. Random Forest and Logistic Regression models were compared with prevalence and BLASTp baselines and tested on an independent 1,378-protein external cohort.

The nucleotide/codon Random Forest achieved the highest internal area under the precision-recall curve (AUPRC) of 0.7889. Feature integration did not show a statistically clear improvement, so the integration hypothesis was not supported. Externally, the integrated Random Forest achieved an AUPRC of 0.4771 versus 0.1421 for BLASTp. PHAGOS then screened a separate PhaRBP harvest beginning with 127,751 raw records and identified 20 metadata-vetted, sequence-dissimilar computational RBP candidates.

PHAGOS narrows large host-unassigned RBP collections into experimentally testable hypotheses rather than confirmed A. baumannii-infecting phages.

Objectives

1

Determine whether sequence-derived RBP characteristics can distinguish A. baumannii-associated RBPs from comparison-host RBPs.

2

Develop and compare Logistic Regression and Random Forest using similarity-aware validation and BLASTp / prevalence baselines.

3

Test finalized models on an independent known-host external dataset without retraining.

4

Screen host-unassigned RBPs and prioritize the strongest biologically plausible candidates for future testing.

Methodology

From curated reference proteins to a frozen, ranked shortlist.

  1. 1

    Dataset Curation

    886 known-host RBPs; 64 A. baumannii positives.

  2. 2

    Feature Extraction

    149 nucleotide/codon + 27 amino-acid + 9 physicochemical = 185 integrated features.

  3. 3

    Similarity Control

    Primary 80% identity grouping; nested grouped cross-validation, 4 outer × 3 inner folds.

  4. 4

    Model Development

    Logistic Regression, Random Forest, prevalence baseline, leakage-controlled BLASTp.

  5. 5

    Independent Validation

    Frozen models tested on 1,378 known-host proteins without retraining.

  6. 6

    Discovery Screening

    PhaRBP harvest metadata audit → CDS quality → labeled-reference similarity screen → CD-HIT80 redundancy control → frozen ranking → biological characterization.

Conclusion

  • PHAGOS successfully developed a similarity-aware, independently validated framework for prioritizing possible A. baumannii-associated RBPs.
  • Nucleotide/codon features provided the strongest individual predictive signal. Integrated RF generalized best externally and clearly exceeded BLASTp on the independent cohort.
  • PHAGOS reduced 127,751 raw records to 20 traceable computational candidates; DISC05843 and DISC06024 were the strongest complementary priorities.

Recommendations

1

Experimentally validate DISC05843 and DISC06024 using host-range, adsorption, and infectivity assays across multiple A. baumannii strains.

2

Expand the number and diversity of experimentally characterized A. baumannii-associated RBPs.

3

Explore structure-aware or protein-language-model features while still preserving similarity-aware validation.

4

Apply the PHAGOS workflow to other clinically important bacterial targets after appropriate retraining and validation.
